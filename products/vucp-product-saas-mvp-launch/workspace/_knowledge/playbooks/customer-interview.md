# Customer Interview Playbook

## Pre-Interview (30 min before)

### 1. Research the Person
- LinkedIn profile
- Recent posts/tweets
- Company website
- Role and responsibilities

### 2. Prepare Questions (5-7 max)
- Based on their specific context
- Open-ended, no leading
- Prioritized by importance

### 3. Test Tech
- Zoom/Meet works
- Recording setup (ask permission!)
- Backup: phone recording

### 4. Set Intentions
- "I'm here to learn, not to sell"
- "No wrong answers"
- "Your honesty helps me build something useful"

## During Interview (30 min)

### Opening (2 min)
"Thanks for taking the time. I'm building something in [space] and your perspective would be invaluable. This is purely research — I'm not selling anything. I'd love to understand how you currently handle [problem]."

### Exploration (20 min)
Follow this flow:
1. **Context:** "Tell me about your role and what a typical day looks like."
2. **Problem:** "When did you last experience [specific problem]?"
3. **Current solution:** "What did you do? What tools?"
4. **Gaps:** "What wasn't perfect about that?"
5. **Cost:** "What did that cost you?" (time, money, stress, opportunity)
6. **Ideal:** "If you had a magic wand?"
7. **Willingness:** "Have you paid for solutions to this before?"

### Closing (5 min)
- "Can I follow up when I have something to show?"
- "Do you know 2-3 others with this problem?"
- "Anything I should have asked?"
- "Thank you — this is incredibly helpful."

## Post-Interview (15 min)

### Immediate Documentation
Fill this template:
```
Date: 
Name: 
Role: 
Company: 

Top 3 Quotes:
1. "..."
2. "..."
3. "..."

Pain Level (1-10): 
Current Solution: 
Willingness to Pay: 
Referrals Given: 

Key Insight: 
Action Item: 
```

### Pattern Tracking
After 3+ interviews, look for:
- Common phrases (use their words in copy!)
- Common pain points
- Common current solutions
- Common gaps

## Red Flags During Interview
- You talk > 20% of time
- They say "that sounds interesting" (not "I need this")
- They can't describe their current process
- They haven't tried to solve the problem before
- They want to give you feature ideas instead of describing pain

## Green Flags
- They interrupt you to describe their pain
- They use emotional language ("frustrating", "nightmare", "waste")
- They already pay for partial solutions
- They introduce you to others unprompted
- They ask "when can I try this?"
